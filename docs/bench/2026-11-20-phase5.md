### What the first tracker needed beyond the spike

Each from a failing test; each is a named constant in `lib/dsp/dsp/Tracker.h` and in the PRD.

1. **A floor of 5 ms on confidence's smoothing.** Over four periods of 6 kHz (0.7 ms) the
   confidence of noise jitters across 30 %; every crossing locked, held, froze and released the
   loop, and a sine at 6 kHz took 0.8 to 1 s to lock.
2. **A jump of the input.** After a sine stops, the two smoothed powers decay together and hold
   confidence up for τ ln(SNR): 35 ms at 40 dB, the spike's figure. Meanwhile the loop, normalised to
   the level, slid 400 cents in 40 ms on the noise left. Now when the input's power over one period
   is 10 dB from the smoothed one, both follow at one period until they are within 1 dB, and a
   locked loop stops: held 7 ms became held 208 ms.
3. **A lock is confirmed** over a quarter of Speed. At 0 dB SNR confidence crosses 30 % for an
   instant some 200 cents short of the sine; locked there, the next dip held and froze the loop on
   its way, and a lock at 1 kHz took 380 ms. Slowing the loop while held instead of freezing it
   was tried and rejected: on noise the loop slides fast enough that a twentieth of it still moved
   the estimate 185 cents in a 500 ms gap.

### What several trackers needed beyond the PRD's lines

1. **Graded weights.** With only the locked deflated (`lock_j` as 0 or 1), eight equal sines leave
   every tracker at a whole-key confidence of 1/8 and none ever locks: the eight bands stayed at
   rest. Now a tracker's weight in the others' deflation is its confidence over the threshold, at
   most 1, and 1 while locked; all eight lock.
2. **The first keeps its component.** Two trackers acquiring one sine together both locked, the
   second from 235 cents away while the first's extraction was still off centre, and the spacing
   then held them straddling it 130 cents apart. A lock is now refused near a locked or held
   tracker, and of two locked or held that close, the more recent is released.
3. **The spacing pushes only the released.** A released tracker acquiring the same sine pushed the
   locked one 70 cents off it, before any onset.
4. **The onset gate is the jump.** No separate gate was needed: the quiet tracker's input jumps by
   24 dB, its loop stops, its confidence falls and it holds, while the newcomer's band is seeded,
   locks, and takes the newcomer out of its input. The spike's worst case went from 726 cents to 6.

- **The identity skipped per sample** (Phase 5's first commit) costs +5 % with every ramp running
  and +3 % on eight dynamic bands, after the helper stopped making MSVC reload the coefficients
  after each store (+21 % before).
- **The static and dynamic paths measure 6 to 8 % above Phase 4.** The machine moves such figures
  by as much from one session to the next (Phase 4's note: unlinked at 64 channels, 1.5 cores one
  day and 1.0 the next), so this is a record, not yet a finding; Phase 7 measures before and after.
- **Track doubles the cost of a dynamic band** (5.0 % against 2.2 %): per tracker per sample, a tan
  and an exp2 at its estimate, two SVFs, a divide for the normalisation, and with eight trackers the
  deflation and the spacing across all pairs. The seeding bank is 0.72 % per key and lane. The
  first suspects for Phase 7: the per-sample tan and exp2 (a first-order update of g would do), and
  the bank (the devplan's first remedy: run it only while a tracker is released).
