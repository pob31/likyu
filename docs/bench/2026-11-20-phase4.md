# Bench note, Phase 4

2026-11-20, Windows 11, Intel Core Ultra 7 255H, MSVC (Visual Studio 18), the suites in Debug and
`likyu_bench` in Release. What Phase 4 measured, beside the rows it turned green. The figures
behind each threshold are printed by the tests that hold them (`MESSAGE` lines in
`tests/dsp/TransientTests.cpp`, `ChainTests.cpp`, `MovementTests.cpp`).

The time constants are the PRD's starting values throughout (1 and 15 ms, 20 and 200 ms, Time 1×),
until the listening pass sets them.

## The transient shaper (4a)

Three one-poles in dB per lane, as the author decided on 2026-11-20 (the attack pair releases at
the fast release, so the PRD's two fast envelopes are one): E_fast (1 ms, 20 ms), E_slowAttack
(15 ms, 20 ms), E_slowRelease (1 ms, 200 ms), on max(L, floor), the gate reading E_fast.

| What | Measured | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attack signal on a 40 dB rise, amount ±50 %, Time 0.5×, 1×, 2× | peaks at 1.45, 2.90, 5.80 ms as the one-poles say; the peak within 0.0002 dB, its time within 0.5 % | sign and timing (PRD); 0.25 dB and 10 % here |
| Sustain signal on a 30 dB fall, the same | peaks at 25.6, 51.2, 102.3 ms; within 0.0002 dB and 0.004 % | the same |
| A rise has no sustain signal, a fall no attack signal | exactly 0, both | none: what the shared constants give |
| Range as the limit | exactly ±\|Range\|, whatever its sign | the PRD's clamp |
| Levels under the floor, any amounts, Time 0.25× and 4×, floors −60 and −30 dB | the offset exactly 0 | "nothing moves below the floor" |
| End to end: a 1 kHz burst through a Transient bell at its centre, Range 12 dB | attack ±12 dB, the full Range, 0.40 ms after the onset, under 0.5 dB 80 ms in; sustain under 0.05 dB while the tone holds, ±12 dB 6.8 ms after the stop, exactly 0 from 250 ms after it | sign and timing (PRD) |
| End to end: a burst at −70 dB under a −60 dB floor | the input, bit for bit, float and double | the same |
| **After silence**: 5 s of silence, then three bursts 1 s apart over a room at −80 dB | attack: 20.6215 dB for 13.6 ms, first and later alike; sustain: 10.6826 against 10.6813 dB, 232.8 ms each | 1 dB and 10 % (PRD, *proposed*) |
| The same on L instead of max(L, floor) (negative control) | 21.9 dB against 16.1 dB | must miss, and does by 5.8 dB |
| Amounts and floor stepped: the largest move of the offset in one sample | 0.0038 dB (sustain flipped), 0.014 dB (floor raised past the level); with one-step ramps, 0.22 and 0.78 dB | none |
| **Exact bypass, return**: eight Transient bands at 0 dB, Range 6 dB, a loud passage, then a key under the floor | the input again, bit for bit, 159 ms after the key fell; stated 169 ms; -0 and denormals, float and double, blocks of 64, 1 and random | the stated time |
| Exact bypass, Transient idle: amounts at 0 on loud noise, or full amounts on a key 20 dB under the floor | the input, bit for bit | the PRD's row |
| Gain limit: ±30 dB static, Range ±30 dB, both amounts pushing the same way | the static band itself, bit for bit: the sum is clamped at the static gain | ±30 dB |

- **The stated time of the return** comes from the gate, not from the signals. Once the key is
  under the floor every envelope runs on the floor, and the offset is at most |Range| times the
  gate, which is under 3 u² for u = (E_fast − floor) / W. E_fast falls with the fast release from
  wherever the key left it, so the offset is under the return to zero's 0.0001 dB after
  (fast release × Time / 2) · ln(3 |Range| (P − floor)² / (W² · 0.0001)), P the level the key falls
  from. With P = +6 dB, over any level the band-pass of this noise reaches, that is 169 ms; the
  bands came back at 159 ms.
- **The first burst after silence is shaped as the others** because the envelopes start where the
  floor is, whether the quiet before was digital silence or a room at −80 dB. On L itself they
  start at −120 dB before the first burst and at the room before the next, and the first attack
  is 5.8 dB larger.

## The switches (4a)

On the 500 Hz sine of Phase 3's switches, a bell at 700 Hz, Q 2, +6 dB static, Range 12 dB. What
goes above 8 kHz around the switch, relative to the sine.

Dynamic to and from Transient, on the steady sine, beside the same two steady gains switched
between two samples (a steady sine has no attack and no sustain, so its Transient offset is 0):

| Switch | Steady gain before, after | Through the chain | Switched hard |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gain mode, Dynamic to Transient | −6, +6 dB | −97.5 dB | −14.8 dB |
| Gain mode, Transient to Dynamic | +6, −6 dB | −97.6 dB | −10.4 dB |

While the shaper is at work: the same sine, its level swung by 6 dB three times a second, so that
the offset moves before and after the switch (up to 3.7 dB):

| Switch | Through the chain |
| --- | --- |
| Gain mode, Static to Transient | **−85.3 dB** |
| Gain mode, Transient to Static | −99.6 dB |
| Sustain, +100 % to −100 % | −88.2 dB |
| Attack, −100 % to +100 % | −99.6 dB |
| Floor, −60 to −20 dB, inside the swing | −99.6 dB |
| Time, 1× to 4× | −99.6 dB |

- **Static to Transient is the loudest switch so far,** and still under the −70 dB of the
  *Modulation safety* row. In Static the band's detector does not run, so it starts cold at the
  switch, and the note the band enters on reads as an onset: an attack of the full Range, 12 dB,
  under the 10 ms fade in. Dynamic to Transient keeps the detector running and the envelopes start
  at its level, which is why that switch is as quiet as any other. **For the listening pass:**
  whether that false attack is heard when a band is put into Transient mid-note.
- **The sustain flip** moves the offset from about +3.7 to −3.7 dB in the 20 ms ramp: −88 dB.

## The lookahead in the chain (4b)

| What | Measured | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idle bands, or the instance bypassed, with each lookahead at 48 and 96 kHz | the input delayed, bit for bit, -0 and denormals, float and double | the PRD's bypass rule |
| Constant latency: an impulse through each type alone and all eight bands in each gain mode, both keys, per lookahead, bypassed or not, 44.1 and 96 kHz (160 cases) | the first non-zero sample at the delay, every time | the PRD's row |
| Block-size invariance with the delay changed three times mid-step (3 ms, 0.25 ms, Off, 1 ms) | bit for bit at blocks of 1, 7, 64, 128, 4096 and random | the PRD's row |

**Lookahead effect.** A 3 kHz burst (0.5, instant onset) through a Transient bell, attack −100 %,
Range 12 dB. The energy of the burst's first stretch at the output, against the same stretch going
in. Beside it, a static bell already at −12 dB: the most any lookahead can give, since a bell's
band-pass takes a few samples to answer a sudden sine.

| First | Without lookahead | With 1 ms | Static bell at −12 dB |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.125 ms | −0.4 dB | −5.3 dB | −5.3 dB |
| 0.25 ms | −1.9 dB | −6.5 dB | −6.5 dB |
| 0.5 ms | −4.9 dB | −8.7 dB | −8.7 dB |
| 1 ms | −7.1 dB | −10.0 dB | −10.0 dB |
| 2 ms | −8.9 dB | −10.9 dB | −10.9 dB |

With 1 ms the band is the static bell to every digit: the offset is at the full cut, clamped,
before the delayed burst arrives. Without, the edge passes and the cut catches up within the first
half millisecond. The same through the processor, in float buffers.

## The plugin (4b, 4c)

- **The key is the host's buffer itself.** The chain reads each stretch of it before it delays and
  writes that stretch, so the shim's copy and its 256-sample chunks are gone. One block of 6000
  samples, 94 times the size announced to `prepareToPlay`, still gives what blocks of 64 give, bit
  for bit, with a lookahead.
- **The temporary transient constants reach every band.** A slow attack of 60 ms instead of 15
  cuts more of a burst through a Transient bell, attack −100 %, Range 30 dB: −22.2 dB over its
  first 30 ms against −13.6 dB. The latency stays 0, and the state chunk does not name them.

- **Transient costs what Dynamic costs.** Most of a moving band is its coefficients, every sample,
  in both modes; the shaper's three one-poles in double weigh about as much as the gain computer,
  its knee and its one-pole.
- **The lookahead's line costs some 4 % of the static EQ** (0.39 to 0.41 %) and nothing
  measurable beside a dynamic or Transient band. It runs at every setting, Off included, so that
  turning the lookahead on reads real history; 3 ms costs what Off costs.
- **Unlinked at 64 channels measured one core today, from both builds.** Phase 3's note gave 1.5
  cores for the same code. The machine's state moves that figure (power plan, thermal state, what
  else was running), not the code. The PRD now gives both.
- **The budget's case** (8 bands in Dynamic and Track, stereo sidechain, 96 kHz, 64 samples) is
  at 2 % before Track. The same day the author replaced the proposed 1 % with "optimised as much
  as possible": there is no fixed figure to meet.
- **The worst block is still Windows' scheduling,** on an ordinary thread: up to 74 % of a period
  once on a run whose median was 2.1 %, 81 % on one at 3.3 %, 89 % on a 64-channel run at 12 %.
