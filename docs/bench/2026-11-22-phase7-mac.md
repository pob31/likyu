# Bench note, Phase 7: the Mac's own pass (7e)

2026-11-22, on the Mac mini (Apple M4 Pro, macOS 15.8.3, Apple clang 17), from `c80858e` to
`921f0b9`. Asked for by the author after the stop note. Every build measured here gives the same
bits as the default arm64 build, which are those of `0c86fad`.

## What was tried, and what came of it

| Tried | Bits | Figure | Kept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `-mcpu=apple-m1` | the same | the budget case 15.1 → 16.0 µs, the others within ±5 % | no |
| `-mcpu=apple-m4` | the same | 15.1 → 15.8 µs | no |
| The reference build: the scalar kernels, which Apple clang vectorises by itself | the same | stereo within 5 to 7 % of the NEON intrinsics; 64 channels 30 to 60 % slower (`static.64` 17.9 → 29.5 µs, `track.64` 33.4 → 45.8) | the intrinsics stay |
| The detectors two key channels at a time (`921f0b9`, for every platform) | the same | M4: within its spread, below; NUC: `trackself.64` 77.6 → 65.4 µs, `dynself.64` 58.4 → 54.0; Windows: 69.8 → 66.7, 58.4 → 56.5; stereo unchanged on both | yes |
| The kernels' widths on NEON (four doubles as two registers, or one register of two) | | not tried: a kernel's four doubles are already two independent registers on NEON, and what a regrouping could gain is smaller than the M4's spread | |

**The stereo regression of the detectors in registers** (2 to 4 % on the M4, from
[`2026-11-22-phase7-kernels.md`](2026-11-22-phase7-kernels.md)) is not clearly mended: two key
channels at a time made `dynself` 7.5 → 6.7 µs and `dynamic` 6.7 → 7.6 µs in the same interleaved
rounds, both inside the spread below. Sampled by time, the detectors are 10 % of `dynamic` on the
M4; the coefficients along the samples are 40 % and each band's level, gain computer and one-pole
30 %. What is left to take there is the coefficients', on every platform.

## The M4's spread

On the M4 the same build reads up to 12 % apart from one run to the next (`dynamic` 6.6 then 7.6
µs; `track` 15.4 then 16.9), and the first run after a build reads high (the budget case 19.0 µs,
then 15.3 to 15.5 in five runs). The Windows laptop's spread is of the same order once warm; the
NUC's is under 2 %. A difference under 10 % on the M4 needs interleaved runs and is still not a
finding.

## The efficiency cores

`likyu_bench suite --core e`, which on macOS asks for the background QoS: the only way a process
reaches the efficiency cores, and the lowest priority macOS has.

| Case | Performance cores | Efficiency cores, background QoS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `static` | 1.4 µs (0.21 %) | 11.2 µs (1.69 %) |
| `dynamic` | 6.6 µs (1.00 %) | 18.1 µs (2.72 %) |
| **`track`** | **15.2 µs (2.28 %)** | **56.8 µs (8.52 %)**, worst block 5.8 ms |
| `trackself` | 13.0 µs (1.96 %) | 41.6 µs (6.23 %) |
| `static.64` | 18.0 µs (2.70 %) | 70.9 µs (10.6 %) |
| `track.64` | 33.6 µs (5.04 %) | 176.7 µs (26.5 %) |

For information: a host's audio thread runs at a real-time priority, not this one, and the worst
blocks here (several milliseconds) are the scheduler's background class, not the code.
