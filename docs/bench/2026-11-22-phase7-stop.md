2026-11-22, at `566d20b`. Stage 7f of the plan: what the remedies that change the numbers would
gain, measured on a throwaway branch (`exp/first-order-g`, local, not pushed), and the universal
binary's x86_64 slice, measured. **Nothing here is on `main`.** Each item ends with a proposal;
each is the author's to decide.

Where it stands without them ([`2026-11-22-phase7-kernels.md`](2026-11-22-phase7-kernels.md)):
the budget case is 2.96 % of a core on the Windows machine, 2.28 % on the M4 Pro, 2.85 % on the
Ryzen, against 4.93, 3.23 and 6.11 % at the baseline, the output unchanged since `0c86fad`.

## 1. The tracker's g to first order between grid points

Each tracker computes g = tan(π 2^E / fs) at its estimate E every sample, and its next estimate
depends on it: 2^x, tan and a divide sit in the chain that carries a tracker from one sample to the
next. The remedy keeps, per tracker, g, E and the slope of g in E at an anchor, and between
anchors takes g = g₀ + slope (E − E₀), one fused multiply-add. An anchor is made at each grid point
and wherever the estimate jumps (a release, a seed); outside the clamped range the slope is 0.

**The suite passes, every threshold.** The Track tests' measured figures, against `main`:

## 2. The seeding bank only while a tracker of its key is not locked

**No figure of the suite moves, and the budget case does not gain** (Windows 19.0 → 19.1 µs; Mac
+2 % over the first-order g, NUC −4 %): its eight trackers are not all locked at every moment, so
the bank still runs, and the test costs a pass over the group every stretch. The bank in
registers is 0.7 to 0.9 µs a block on the three machines; that is all this could ever save.

**Proposed:** no. It would also leave the bank cold at the moment a tracker lets go, which is when
the bank is read.

## 3 and 4. The pair checks at control rate; the rotated deflation

Not measured, and not proposed. The pair checks run only when a released tracker may lock or two
estimates are within the spacing (`fbef822`); otherwise they are one pass over eight estimates per
sample. The rotated deflation would save each tracker its extraction filter, which runs now with
the other members' in one register: one filter's chain for the eight. Neither has much left to
take.

## 5. The universal binary's x86_64 slice

The installers will build a universal binary for macOS. Its x86_64 slice runs on Intel Macs, and
on Apple silicon in a host that runs as x86_64, through Rosetta. Measured on the M4 Pro,
macOS 15.8.3, under `arch -x86_64`:

| x86_64 slice | Under Rosetta | The budget case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AVX2 with FMA, as on Windows and Linux | **runs**: 179 cases pass, `mulAdd` fused, the registers' tests pass | 56.0 µs (8.39 %) |
| `LIKYU_SIMD=scalar` (no AVX2, no FMA) | runs, 179 cases pass, its own arithmetic | 29.4 µs (4.42 %) |
| (the arm64 slice, for comparison) | native | 15.2 µs (2.28 %) |

- Rosetta before macOS 15 does not translate AVX2 (as Apple documented it; not measured here): an
  AVX2 slice would not load in an x86_64 host on Apple silicon there. The deployment target is
  macOS 11.
- On an Intel Mac, the AVX2 slice needs a Haswell or later (2013); the 2013 Mac Pro has none.
- Under Rosetta the scalar slice is twice as fast as the AVX2 one; on a real x86 the AVX2 build is
  the faster (the NUC's scalar build was 1.5 to 2 times slower before the kernels).

**Proposed:** the scalar build for the x86_64 slice of the macOS installer. It loads on every
Intel Mac and in every x86_64 host on Apple silicon whatever the macOS, and it is the faster under
Rosetta. Intel Macs are few and old, and their users lose least by it. The Windows and Linux
installers stay AVX2, as decided. The slice is then its own arithmetic; its suite passes.

## 6. For the Mac's own pass (7e), which may wait

- **The detectors in registers cost the M4 2 to 4 % in stereo** (they gain 20 to 26 % on wide
  layouts keyed by the input). The M4's out-of-order window was already overlapping the sixteen
  scalar chains. Candidates: the four-float width on NEON for that kernel, or the scalar loop under
  three key channels on arm64.
- The other items of 7e stand as planned: `-mcpu`, Apple clang's vectoriser on the scalar back-end,
  the efficiency cores.
