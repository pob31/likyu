# Bench note, Phase 2

2026-11-20. What the static EQ measures: the precision of its audio path (devplan 2d), its first
timing (2f), and the figures behind the thresholds this phase proposed or confirmed. The machine is
the author's Windows bench machine, Intel Core Ultra 7 255H, 32 GB, Windows 11, MSVC 14.51 (VS
2026), Release, unless a line says otherwise. The farm did not answer over SSH on the day.

## Precision of the audio path (devplan 2d)

One band at 20 Hz, Q 40, 192 kHz, the case the PRD names. Each precision runs the band with its
states **and** its coefficients in that precision, against the same band in `long double`. The
error is in dB relative to the band's output, over the second half of a 10 s run
(`likyu_bench precision`).

| Band | Input | float | double |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bell +12 dB | full-scale noise | −94.2 dB | −262.9 dB |
| Bell +12 dB | 1 kHz sine, −20 dBFS | −134.5 dB | −302.6 dB |
| Bell −12 dB | full-scale noise | −113.3 dB | −299.9 dB |
| Bell −12 dB | 1 kHz sine, −20 dBFS | −146.9 dB | −326.0 dB |
| Low-pass | full-scale noise | −47.7 dB | −224.8 dB |
| Low-pass | 1 kHz sine, −20 dBFS | −53.8 dB | −240.7 dB |

The float column is the same from Windows and from CI's Linux job. The double column is from CI's
Linux job, run 44700763094 (GCC on x86-64, `long double` of 64 mantissa bits); MSVC's `long double`
is `double` and cannot measure it.

**What the float figures are.** Not noise in the usual sense: at 20 Hz and 192 kHz, `g` is 3.3e-4
and `a1 = 1 / (1 + g (g + k))` is 1 − 8.3e-6. A float holds that difference to about 0.7 %, so the
float filter's damping, and with it its Q, is off by that much: a different filter, −48 dB from the
intended one at the output of a low-pass. A bell hides most of it under its direct term.

**Conclusion.** The PRD's decision of 2026-11-16 stands: states and coefficients in double, whose
error is 225 dB or more under the output at worst. Float would cost the low bands their tuning at
high rates.

## First timing (devplan 2f)

`likyu_bench time`: the chain's process call alone, back to back after 200 blocks of warm-up, 10 s
of audio. Stereo, 96 kHz, blocks of 64 samples (a period of 666.7 µs), float buffers unless said.

| Scenario | Median | p99 | p999 | Mean | Median, share of one core |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 static bands | 2.4 µs | 2.5–3.4 µs | 5.1–7.9 µs | 2.42 µs | **0.36 %** |
| 8 bands, every ramp always running | 5.9 µs | 8.3 µs | 17.7 µs | 5.95 µs | 0.89 % |
| 8 static bands, double buffers | 3.0 µs | 4.8 µs | 12.5 µs | 3.09 µs | 0.45 % |
| 8 static bands, 64 channels (information) | 68.5 µs | 183 µs | 326 µs | 73.5 µs | 10.3 % |

- *Static* is eight bands on, of mixed types, none at 0 dB. *Every ramp always running* retargets
  every band's frequency, Q and gain every block, so that coefficients are computed every sample all
  the time: the worst case of a static EQ.
- **Not included:** the shim's reading of 284 parameters per block and the lookahead delay. No
  detection exists yet: the PRD's budget case (Dynamic and Track, stereo sidechain) is Phase 7's.
- **The worst block is not a DSP figure here.** It ranged from 52 to 129 µs (8 to 19 % of the
  period) between identical runs, and reached 1.1 ms at 64 channels: the bench runs on an ordinary
  thread and these are Windows' scheduling. The bench against the budget (Phase 7) should run at
  audio priority, as WFS-DIY's `pipeline-bench` does.
- **For the author: the 1 % budget** (PRD, open decision). The static EQ takes about a third of it,
  before any detector, envelope or tracker. Phase 7's SIMD is planned for the detection lanes,
  which is where most of the rest will be.

## Denormals, the timing half

Same bench, scenario `decay`: 5 s on noise, then 5 s of silence. Median 2.4 µs in both halves
(silence over signal: 1.000); the worst block lower in silence (8.4 µs) than with signal
(12.0 µs). Flush-to-zero comes from `lib/dsp` itself (`dsp/Denormals.h`).

Found on the way: flush-to-zero alone does not end a decay in this structure. Once the products
underflow, `ic = 2 v − ic` flips the sign of a state forever at the edge of the normal range (a
constant 1.6e-307 out, measured). That is not slow, since these are normal numbers, but the output
never reaches 0. The chain sets a state under 1e-20 to 0 at the end of each control step.

## Thresholds measured

| What | Measured | Threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tan, 20 Hz to 20 kHz (row *tan approximation*) | under 1e-6 cent below 88.2 kHz (rational form); 0.0004 cent from 88.2 kHz (polynomial) | 1 cent (PRD) |
| Static response, 8 types × 6 rates × 3 settings, to 0.4 fs | 0.0005 dB at worst (a notch beside its zero) | 0.1 dB (PRD) |
| Static response, Q 40 at ±30 dB, 48 kHz | 1.3e-11 dB at worst | 0.1 dB (PRD) |
| Block-size invariance, 1 to 4096 and random sizes | bit for bit | bit for bit (PRD, proposed: confirmed) |
| Switch clicks, above 8 kHz, relative to a 500 Hz sine | −81 dB at worst (bell to low shelf); −88 to −99 dB for the others; the bypass −87.5 dB. A linear crossfade measured −52 dB, a hard switch −0.5 to −42 dB | −70 dB (proposed here) |
| `g` interpolated over 16 samples, against the log glide | 44 cents at worst, near 20 kHz at 44.1 kHz on a 20 Hz → 20 kHz ramp in 20 ms, inside steps of about 217 cents; 0.036 cent on a one-octave ramp at 1 kHz | none; the 16 is confirmed by modulation safety, as the PRD asks |
| Modulation safety, a new random target every sample on a 0.5 sine | peak 1.16 | no NaN, no growth |

The switch meter is a fourth-order high-pass at 8 kHz on the output, which leaves the 500 Hz test
sine 96 dB down; a click is broadband and shows there (`tests/dsp/MovementTests.cpp`). The
smoothstep crossfade replaced a linear one when the linear one measured −52 dB: the corners of a
linear fade are what it sprays.
